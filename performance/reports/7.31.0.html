Yuneta 7.31.0 against 7.30.0

7.31.0 closes a cycle of 26 releases and 18 rounds of review against 7.30.0: the agent, the control center, the MQTT broker and gates, the transports and the inter-yuno events were hardened against peers that lie, flood or do not read; the deprecated C_PROT_MQTT was removed. Measured against 7.30.0, the last release with a study, before the tag: none of the 66 figures moved outside its noise. The hardening costs nothing that these benchmarks can see.

How it was measured

7.31.0 against 7.30.0. Written on 2026-11-13, before the tag. 7.31.0 is the C code of main at 22199d4b8, measured against 7.30.0, the last release with a study; the 26 releases between them (7.30.1 to 7.30.26) were never studied. Both sides are git worktrees (yunetas-perf-7.30.0 and yunetas-perf-head) built by yunetas init --sdk-only and make install of the kernel libraries and modules into their own outputs/, the benchmarks and the three ctest binaries from their root build/; outputs_ext is a copy of the main tree's (linux-ext-libs 1.28 on both sides, no change in kernel/c/linux-ext-libs between them). the binaries pinned to one CPU (taskset -c 6) and run in rotation, the order turned each round, a sync and a 3 s pause (1 s for the three ctest binaries) before each run: 10 rounds. Mean +- standard deviation (median), t = the change over its standard error; |t| < 3 is noise. The BFF figure and the event loop echoes are means of the samples of a run, the first left out (and the last, for the echoes). The treedb figures (perf_tr_treedb, perf_tr_treedb_read) are CPU time of the process; perf_c_treedb is wall time. The page-by-page read rate is the mean of the FORWARD and BACKWARD passes of test_topic_pkey_integer_iterator5. perf_http and perf_micro are drivers written AGAIN for this study from what 7.30.0.json says they measured (the drivers of that study were not kept): their A/B holds, their absolute figures do not compare with the 7.30.0 report. No hardware counters: perf_event_paranoid is 4 on this machine and no root, so no figure was outside its noise, so none needed one. The rounds ran while other work of the machine waited for them (the same lock), with a load average of 1 to 2.

Absolute figures. The absolute figures of 7.31.0 are its side of the A/B run (main at 22199d4b8), measured on 2026-11-13. The binary sizes are those of the yunos of that worktree, built with the same .config (make, not installed). The commits after 22199d4b8 and before the tag change only the control center, ycli and ycommand, on no measured path.

Machine. 11th Gen Intel Core i7-11370H @ 3.30 GHz, 4 cores / 8 threads (up to 4.8 GHz), 31 GiB RAM, Samsung SSD 990 PRO 2TB (NVMe) (ext4), Linux 7.0.0-42-generic x86_64, Ubuntu 26.04.1 LTS, glibc 2.43, gcc 15.2.0. Acer Nitro AN517-53. A development machine, at rest for this study: every round ran under the lock of the measurements; the two agents of the machine, a logcenter and three gates of an application stayed up, idle..

Build. RelWithDebInfo (-O2 -g -DNDEBUG), gcc 15.2.0, fully static: yes, TLS: OpenSSL 3.6.5 (bundled, static: linux-ext-libs 1.28); mbedTLS not built on this machine. CONFIG_DEBUG_TRACK_MEMORY is on (the .config of this machine): every allocation is tracked. A build without it does less work per allocation; that build was not measured here.

The benchmarks are in performance/c/; the raw figures of this page are in 7.31.0.json; every A/B, change by change, is in performance/c/README.md and in the CHANGELOG.
