Yuneta 7.27.5: retention, and what it costs a replica to read

7.27.5 gives timeranger2 retention: a store deletes the old files of a key and keeps the g_rowid of every row that stays, and its followers hear the purge. This study compares it with 7.27.0, the last release with a study, so it covers 7.27.1 to 7.27.5 together, and every move is placed in the release where it appeared by building the tags between them. It replaces the one written the day after the release against 7.27.4, a release nobody had measured. A replica pays about 1 us more for every iterator open and every page, purged or not: one stat() that notices a purge whose signal it missed. That is +18% on a page of one row, +7% on an open; the master does not pay it. It appeared in 7.27.5, with commit 83a771089. A page of 100 rows costs 1.5% more (the base of the key read for every row), and a forced treedb delete 3.9% more. Everything else from 7.27.1 to 7.27.5, the gobj and TCP changes of 7.27.2 to 7.27.4 among them, moves inside its noise.

Prices paid on purpose

What got slower against 7.27.0, how much, in which release it appeared, and what it buys.

A page of 1 row, as a replica

+18.0%

5.25 µs

6.20 µs

check_purge_before_read(): before every iterator open and every page, a replica stat()s the first md2 file of the key, to notice a purge of the master whose signal it did not hear (an overflow of its watch, a follower down while the master purged, a replica without a follower), before the read meets files that are gone. About 1 us per call on this machine, purged or not. Placed by a rotation of every tag from 7.27.0 to 7.27.5 and of two commits of 7.27.5 (24 rounds): 7.27.1 to 7.27.4 and bd3fdce51 read at 7.27.0's speed, and it appears with 83a771089 (the followers hear a purge), the commit that brings the check. On this figure: 7.27.4 5.12 us, bd3fdce51 5.19, 83a771089 6.03, 7.27.5 6.10. Kept: skipping the check while nothing seems missed leaves a window (a signal not yet read from the queue) in which a read opens a deleted file.

+3.9% against 7.27.0 (t = 2.5, at the edge of the noise), and it is 7.27.5: with 7.27.4 in the rotation, 7.27.4 42.0 us and 7.27.5 44.5 us (+6.1%, t = 4.5). A forced delete removes the node's key (tranger2_delete_key()), and since 7.27.5 it first looks for the key's base (keys/<key>/.purged, one access()): a key with a base is moved out of keys/ whole, by a rename(), before it is removed, so a crash never leaves its files without its base or its base without its files. Attributed by reading the code.

How it was measured

7.27.5 against 7.27.0. Written on 2026-10-22, after the release, and against 7.27.0: the last release with a study. It replaces the study written the day before, which compared 7.27.5 with 7.27.4, a release nobody had measured. This one covers 7.27.1 to 7.27.5. 7.27.0 and 7.27.5 each built from a git worktree of its tag, with the same .config and compiler; in each tree only the kernel libraries, the modules, performance/c/ and the three ctest binaries were built, and the perf_timeranger2 of the main tree (with its read phase, public API only) was compiled into both. outputs_ext was shared (linux-ext-libs 1.24, OpenSSL 3.6.5 and PCRE2 10.49): 7.27.0 shipped with 1.23 (OpenSSL 3.6.3), and that change of 7.27.1 is not in these figures; it was measured on its own when 7.27.1 shipped (no move: the TLS echo -0.9%, t = -0.6). The two binaries of each benchmark run alternately, the order flipped every round, with a sync and a 3 s pause before each run (1 s for the three ctest binaries and the read phase): 16 rounds; 24 for perf_timeranger2 and perf_tr_treedb, run with 7.27.4 in a rotation of three; 48 for the read phase alone (the last 24 with 7.27.4 in the rotation). perf_rotatory 16 rounds, its 9th left out: something on the machine slowed both builds alike for that minute (a log line 1.5 times its time on both). To place each move in its release, the read phase ran once more, 24 rounds, on every tag from 7.27.0 to 7.27.5 and on two commits of 7.27.5 (bd3fdce51, its first, and 83a771089). Mean +- standard deviation (median), t = the change over its standard error; |t| < 3 is noise. The treedb figures are CPU time of the process per operation.

Absolute figures. The absolute figures of 7.27.5 are its side of this A/B run: the code of the tag, built on 2026-10-22 from a worktree of the tag on the machine of the earlier reports. Not a tag-time run: the release was tagged on 2026-10-21 without one. The per-second benchmarks drop their first and last second; reads page by page are the FORWARD pass. The binary sizes are those of a build of the tag made the same day for the first study.

Machine. 11th Gen Intel Core i7-11370H @ 3.30 GHz, 4 cores / 8 threads (up to 4.8 GHz), 31 GiB RAM, Samsung SSD 990 PRO 2TB (NVMe) (ext4), Linux 7.0.0-38-generic x86_64, Ubuntu 26.04.1 LTS, glibc 2.43, gcc 15.2.0. Acer Nitro AN517-53. A development machine, with other work running at times; the A/B runs are alternated for that reason.

Build. RelWithDebInfo (-O2 -g -DNDEBUG), gcc 15.2.0, fully static: yes, TLS: OpenSSL 3.6.5 (bundled, static: linux-ext-libs 1.24, both sides); mbedTLS not built on this machine. CONFIG_DEBUG_TRACK_MEMORY is on (the .config of this machine): every allocation is tracked. A build without it does less work per allocation; that build was not measured here.
