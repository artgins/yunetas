Yuneta Simplified · performance report · 2026-11-22

Yuneta 7.32.0 against 7.31.1

7.32.0 closes a cycle of 28 lite releases (7.31.2 to 7.31.29, none with a study) and adds its own hardening: a regex that a peer sends to a store has a time bound, the filters of list-agents are bounded PCRE2 patterns, help takes linear time, and the BFF no longer gives one browser the session of another. Measured against 7.31.1, the last release with a study, before the tag: 61 of the 66 figures are inside their noise, three are faster and two are 2.2% to 2.4% slower. Every move was located by release, and none of them is new work per operation: the code on those paths is the code of 7.31.1, compiled and placed otherwise.

New in 7.32.0: an rkey has a time bound

An rkey is a regular expression (PCRE2) that selects the keys of a timeranger2 topic: a GUI that follows some devices sends one, and a peer with read permission can send any. In 7.31.29 a costly pattern held a single-thread yuno for seconds to minutes. 7.32.0 judges each key once, keeps the verdict, and gives each judgement a budget: a first search with 8 frames at each start position, then 2 ms of positions, and 2 s for the whole load of a list. The new rkey phase of perf_timeranger2 measures both ends: what an ordinary append pays, and how a worst case ends. 7.31.1 has no such phase, so these are new figures, with no A/B.

What got slower, and why

Two figures are slower outside their noise, in both runs. Neither buys anything: the source of their paths is the source of 7.31.1, and each one moves with commits that do not touch it, so they are code generation and placement, located by release and by commit below. One price is paid on purpose and these benchmarks do not see it: on a follower, the check of the closed lists. Measured for the CHANGELOG against 7.31.29, 1000 appends read by a follower take 14.9 ms instead of 12.8 with 100 memory lists (+16%), 86.2 ms instead of 79.2 with 1000 (+9%), 48.8 ms instead of 44.2 with 8 disk feeds of 1000 lists (+10%), and 2329 ms instead of 2635 with 10000 lists (faster). It buys a follower whose callbacks can open and close lists without corrupting the batch.

How it was measured

7.32.0 against 7.31.1. Written on 2026-11-22, before the tag. 7.32.0 is the C code of 318204992, its version commit (the release commit after it changes no .c file), measured against 7.31.1, the last release with a study; the 28 lite releases between them (7.31.2 to 7.31.29) were never studied. Between the two sides: 232 commits, 19,323 lines added and 1,166 removed in .c and .h (7,863 and 1,059 outside the tests and the benchmarks). Both sides are git worktrees (yunetas-perf-7.31.1 at 3c53c9075 and yunetas-perf-head) built by yunetas init --sdk-only and make install of the kernel libraries and modules into their own outputs/, the benchmarks and the three ctest binaries from their root build/, the yunos by make. Each side has the linux-ext-libs of its release: 1.28 for 7.31.1, 1.31 for 7.32.0 (jansson 2.15.1-yuneta.2; mbedTLS 4.3.0 and nginx 1.31.9 are not in these binaries). The binaries pinned to one CPU (taskset -c 6) and run in rotation, the order turned each round, a sync and a 3 s pause (1 s for the three ctest binaries) before each run: 10 rounds. Mean +- standard deviation (median), t = the change over its standard error; |t| < 3 is noise. The eight figures outside their noise were measured again, 10 more rounds of their six benchmarks (perf_timeranger2, perf_tr_treedb, perf_auth_bff, perf_tcp_test4, perf_tcps_test4, perf_mqtt), and their reasons give both runs and the 20 rounds together. The losses that held were located by tag: 7.31.24, 7.31.25 and 7.31.29 built in scratch worktrees (each with linux-ext-libs 1.31, as their releases had) and run in one rotation with both sides, 8 rounds; then 7.31.1 with the linux-ext-libs of 7.32.0, and five commits of 7.32.0 (d5feb36c8, 17fc90a4a, 78b98acb5, 60aac358a, 7de398cfb), in one more rotation of 8 rounds. The BFF figure and the event loop echoes are means of the samples of a run, the first left out (and the last, for the echoes). The treedb figures (perf_tr_treedb, perf_tr_treedb_read) are CPU time of the process; perf_c_treedb is wall time. The page-by-page read rate is the mean of the FORWARD and BACKWARD passes of test_topic_pkey_integer_iterator5. perf_http and perf_micro are the drivers written for the 7.31.0 study, kept since. perf_timeranger2 of 7.32.0 has a new phase, rkey: its five figures have no side in 7.31.1 and are given as new figures. No hardware counters (perf_event_paranoid is 4 on this machine and no root). The rounds ran with the machine at rest, a load average of 0.4 to 1.

Absolute figures. The absolute figures of 7.32.0 are its side of the A/B run (318204992), measured on 2026-11-21. The binary sizes are those of the yunos of that worktree, built with the same .config (make, not installed).
