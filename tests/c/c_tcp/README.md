# c_tcp test

Unit tests for the `C_TCP` client GClass. Exercises connect/disconnect cycles, I/O with a local echo server (`pepon`), and timeout handling.

`test12` (`main_test12.c` + `c_test12.c`) is the local stop of a TLS `C_TCP` with TLS output queued. A TLS client (`tcps://`) connects to a listener of the test; the test accepts the connection and gives it to a TLS clisrv (legacy method, with the `ytls` of the test), so both ends are `C_TCP`s of the yuno. With TLS 1.3 the client ends its handshake writing its Finished, and that write is in flight when its `EV_CONNECTED` comes: there the test sends it a message, whose TLS record waits behind the Finished (`dl_tx_encrypted`), and posts the stop to itself, which comes before the completion of the Finished (a posted event is delivered before the next completion). The stop must say ONE INFO (*"tcp data queued at a local stop, dropped"*) with `dropped_msgs` 0, `dropped_bytes` 0 and `dropped_tls_bytes` more than the 26 bytes of the text (48 with OpenSSL), and `txMsgsDroppedAtStop` must stay 0: a TLS record is not a message of the host. Up to 7.30.26 the TLS output went in silence (red: *"the TLS stop does not say the TLS output it drops: lines 0"*). Mutants killed: the INFO only when the clear queue holds data (no INFO), the TLS bytes not added (0), the TLS output counted in `txMsgsDroppedAtStop` (1).

`test11` (`main_test11.c` + `c_test11.c`) is the deadline of the TLS handshake of a CLIENT. Two raw listeners of the test: one drips a TLS record header and its body, one byte each 200 ms; the other accepts and says nothing. A TLS client with `timeout_tls_handshake` 1000 and `timeout_inactivity` 2000 connects to the drip, and must be dropped within 4 s of its accept; up to 7.30.17 each byte read re-armed the timer with the inactivity time, and the drip held the handshake open (red: *"the drip kept the TLS handshake past its deadline"*). Three TLS clients connect to the mute listener at the same time and must be dropped at their deadline (`disconnect_cause` *"TLS: the handshake did not end in time"*): the four deadlines of that window write ONE warning, `"suppressed": 0`. 11 s later, past the window of 10 s, three more clients connect: the first deadline writes the next warning, `"suppressed": 3`, and the two others of that window are said when the yuno ends, in a warning of their own (*"TLS handshakes not ended in time, connections dropped, not said in their window"*, `"suppressed": 2`). Up to 7.30.17 each deadline wrote its own warning (red: *"Unexpected error"*, the second warning); up to 7.30.18 the deadlines after the last warning were said by nobody (red: *"Expected error not consumed"*). Mutants killed: the inactivity timer armed in any state (the drip red), no rate limit (four warnings), the count not counted (`"suppressed": 0` in the second), no count said at the stop (the third warning missing).

`test10` (`main_test10.c` + `c_test10.c`) drops a CLIENT with a write in flight. The `C_TCP` connects to a listener of the test (a 128 KB receive buffer); the test accepts the peer and finds the number of the client's socket by its name. 12 MB in chunks of 1 MB are sent, so a write stays in flight, and the `C_TCP` is dropped (`EV_DROP`): it must wait in `ST_WAIT_STOPPED` with its socket OPEN. Right after the drop a file of the test takes that number if it is free. The peer reads every 20 ms until the end of the connection: the file must get nothing, the peer whole chunks (the write in flight completes; the queued chunks go away with the connection), and the number is closed when `EV_DISCONNECTED` comes. Up to 7.30.17 the drop closed the client's socket at once (the stop of its connect event) and a rest written in `ST_WAIT_STOPPED` went to that number: to the file that took it, or to EBADF (red: *"the socket of a dropped client was closed before its stop ended"*, *"the peer did not get whole chunks"*; on a kernel that completes the write in flight short, also *"the rest of the write of a dropped client went to another file"*).

`test9` (`main_test9.c` + `c_test9.c`) connects a `C_TCP` to a listener of the test, and gives a `C_TCP` clisrv (legacy method: its `fd_clisrv`) a connection the test accepted from a socket of its own, the peer. Both must connect (the clisrv's `EV_CONNECTED` comes inside its start, the client's when its connect completes; red: *"the C_TCPs did not connect"*). Then in ONE turn, to each: a write, two more writes (queued behind the first), the stop (`ST_WAIT_STOPPED`: the write and the cancel of the read in flight) and the DESTROY, before `EV_STOPPED` -- the way every yuno ends, so it must corrupt nothing: the destroy cancels the write, and the loop frees it with no callback. The stop drops the two queued writes and says it: ONE INFO per stop (*"tcp data queued at a local stop, dropped"*, `dropped_msgs` 2, `dropped_bytes` 54, `dropped_tls_bytes` 0; an INFO, the stop is the yuno's own), and `txMsgsDroppedAtStop` 2 (two queued, so a count of the first one alone fails: mutant killed); up to 7.30.26 it went in silence (red: *"TEST: the frame queued at the stop is not counted"*; mutant killed: the line removed). The client's first stop has no destroy: when its `EV_STOPPED` comes it is started again, connects again, and is sent the three writes and stopped again: the count must be 4 (mutant killed: `=` instead of `+=`), and `stats __reset__` must put it back to 0 (mutant killed: no reset in `mt_writing`); only then is it destroyed, with its write in flight. And the destroy closes the clisrv's socket as the stop does, `shutdown(SHUT_WR)` first: between the stop and the destroy the peer sends data nobody reads, and 500 ms later the peer must read the end of the connection, a FIN, not a reset. Up to 7.29.3 the write events were not kept, and the completion of the write ran on the freed gobj (red: a SegFault); and the clisrv's socket was left open (red: *"the socket of a clisrv destroyed before its stop ended was not closed"*); in the fixes after 7.29.3 it was closed with no `shutdown(SHUT_WR)`, and the unread data made it a RST (red: *"the peer of a clisrv destroyed before its stop ended got a reset, not the end"*).

`test8` (`main_test8.c` + `c_test8.c`) is a `C_IOGATE` with `send_type` 1 (send to all), a `C_TCP_S` and 3 channels (`C_PROT_TCP4H` over `C_TCP`), and 3 raw peers. One `EV_SEND_MESSAGE` with a gbuffer to the gate: each peer must read the whole TCP4H frame (its 4-byte length, then the message). Up to 7.25.20 the channels shared the gbuffer, the first read it out, and peers 2 and 3 got an empty frame (red: *"a peer did not get the whole message"*, peers 1 and 2).

`test7` (`main_test7.c` + `c_test7.c`) drops a connected `C_TCP` and, while the drop waits in `ST_WAIT_STOPPED` for its read to be cancelled, sends it data (two messages, 13 bytes) -- what the layers above do until `EV_DISCONNECTED` tells them. The data must go away with no error, as the pending queue of a dead connection does, with ONE warning for the connection (*"tcp data sent while the connection closes, dropped"*, `dropped_msgs` 2, `dropped_bytes` 13), and the stop must end. Then a second `C_TCP` is dropped, sent `gone`, stopped and DESTROYED while it still waits in `ST_WAIT_STOPPED`: its drop must be said too, at the destroy (*"tcp data sent while the connection closes, dropped"*, `dropped_msgs` 1, `dropped_bytes` 4). Last, a third `C_TCP` with `timeout_inactivity` 300 ms connects and stays idle: its `disconnect_cause` must be `Inactivity timeout`, and the first one's `Local dropping`, read at their `EV_DISCONNECTED` and after the close (up to 7.25.20 the cancel of their read, `Operation canceled`, replaced them). Up to 7.25.10 `ST_WAIT_STOPPED` did not declare `EV_TX_DATA`: *"Event NOT DEFINED in state"* per message (red); up to 7.25.20 the data went with no trace at the default levels (red: *"Expected error not consumed"*).

`test6` (`main_test6.c` + `c_test6.c`) connects a `C_TCP` to a port where nobody listens: it waits in `ST_DISCONNECTED` for its reconnect timer, and is stopped there, started again and stopped again. Each stop must publish `EV_STOPPED` once and end in `ST_STOPPED`, and the start after it must work. Up to 7.25.4 this stop published nothing and kept the connect event: the next start failed with *"yev_connect ALREADY exists"* and the client never connected again (red).

`test5` (`main_test5.c` + `c_test5.c`) gives a connected `C_TCP` first an `EV_TX_DATA` with an EMPTY gbuffer: it must be ignored with a warning (*"tcp tx data empty, ignored"*) and the connection must stay up; up to 7.29.3 it dropped the connection (red: *"an empty write dropped the connection"*). With nothing in flight, its `EV_TX_READY` must come once, a turn later and not inside the send; in the fixes after 7.29.3 none came (red: *"an empty write with nothing in flight did not get one EV_TX_READY"*). A second `C_TCP` connects on demand (`timeout_inactivity` 20000, `connect_on_start` false) and stays in `ST_DISCONNECTED`; an empty write there must not connect it, and its `EV_TX_READY` must come once, a turn later; in the fixes after 7.29.3 it came only in `ST_CONNECTED` (red: *"an empty write to a client that connects on demand did not get one EV_TX_READY"*). Then the same gbuffer twice: the second send must answer -1 (*"tcp tx data already queued or in flight, refused"*; red: *"the same gbuffer sent twice was not refused"*). Then a write that does not start: an empty gbuffer to the write of the TLS output (`EV_SEND_ENCRYPTED_DATA`, which no host sends; here it is the way to that path), which `yev_start_event()` refuses with -1, as it does without memory to keep a submission. The connection must be dropped (*"Cannot start a write: the connection is dropped"*, `EV_DISCONNECTED`), the write event and its gbuffer freed, and a stop must reach `ST_STOPPED`. Up to 7.25.4 the -1 was ignored: the connection stayed up, the event and its gbuffer leaked, and the stop waited in `ST_WAIT_STOPPED` for ever (red: *"a write that did not start did not drop the connection"*, *"the stop ... did not end"*, *"system memory not free"*).
